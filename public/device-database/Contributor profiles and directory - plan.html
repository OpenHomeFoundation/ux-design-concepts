Planning · Stage 03 increment

Contributor profiles: a name that goes somewhere

Stage 03 attributes every edit. This increment makes that attribution a person you can click: a public profile with identity and a contributions timeline, and a browsable directory of everyone who tends the database. With it off, an attribution is just a name. This is the plan for turning credit into a profile, without turning a reporting population into a roster.

Builds on Stage 03 · Accounts and editing · lives in contributor-profile.jsx

A profile describes an editor, never a device owner. The people with profiles are the ones who choose to contribute edits, named because attribution is part of the editorial contract. The people who opt in to share what devices they run stay anonymous, forever. This increment makes contributors legible without ever making the reporting population visible, and that line is the whole design.

What it is

Identity, a timeline, and a directory

A profile is two things: identity, the avatar, name, bio, and links, and a contributions timeline. The same component renders your own profile and everyone else's, your own simply adds Settings and Sign out and is reached from the nav avatar. The directory lists contributors so the community is browsable, not just discoverable one edit at a time.

The profile#/contributors/:handle

Identity plus a timeline of edits. Live edits come from each device's history filtered by author, pending edits from the pending store. The timeline is derived, never a second copy.

The directory#/contributors

A browsable index of contributors. Edit attributions across the site link here, so a name on a device page becomes a way to see what else that person has tended.

With the increment off, attributions render as plain names with nowhere to go, and the directory route is simply absent. Nothing else changes, which is what lets profiles be toggled independently of Notifications and Your edits.

The design decisions

Derived, public-by-decision, privacy-gated

Three choices define the increment. The timeline is derived rather than stored, so it cannot drift from the edits it reflects. Pending edits are public on a profile, a deliberate product decision in favour of an open review process. And privacy is a real control, not an afterthought.

SettingWhat it hides
Public profileThe default. Identity, stats, and the full contributions timeline are visible to anyone.
PrivateHides the whole profile from others. You still see your own in full.
Activity hiddenKeeps the header, hides stats and timeline. A name without a public ledger.

You always see your own profile in full regardless of these flags, because the private view is also the surface you manage yourself from. Pending edits showing publicly is the one choice worth restating: it makes review legible, and it is the reason a profile reflects work in flight, not just merged history.

Where we draw the line

Credit for editors, anonymity for everyone else

Profiles are for contributors, not reporters. A profile reflects edits a person chose to make. It never exposes who runs which device. The reporting population stays anonymous, full stop.

Privacy is a control the contributor holds. Private hides the whole profile, activity-hidden keeps the name and drops the ledger. You opt into visibility, you are not opted in by editing.

The timeline is derived, never authored. It is computed from edit history and the pending store, so it cannot claim a contribution that did not happen or hide one that did.

A profile is a record of work, not a leaderboard. It shows what a person tended. It does not rank contributors or gamify edits, which would pull against the neutral, factual register.

Recommendation

What to hold

  1. Keep the editor-versus-reporter line bright. A profile must never become a path to who owns a device. That separation is inherited from Foundation and is non-negotiable.

  2. Derive the timeline, never store it. One source of truth for contributions means a profile can never disagree with the device history it draws from.

  3. Treat privacy flags as first-class. Private and activity-hidden are the contributor's controls. Honour them everywhere a name could otherwise link out.
