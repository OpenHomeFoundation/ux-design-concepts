Notifications: closing the loop on a submitted edit

You submit an edit, then what? This increment tells you: when it opens for review, goes live, or is declined, through the channel you already use, email or GitHub, or off if you would rather just check. One small event model, surfaced where it matters. This is the plan for the loop, and for keeping the review conversation in one place.

Builds on Stage 03 · Accounts and editing · lives in notifications.jsx

Three events, one channel, no noise. A submitted edit can do exactly three things worth telling you about: open as a pull request, get merged live, or be declined. Notifications carry those three and nothing else, on the channel you pick, and the actual review conversation stays on the pull request where everyone can see it. This is a status line, not an inbox to manage.

What it is

One event model, surfaced in two places

There is a single record per event, and it shows up in two surfaces rather than a standalone inbox. Settings owns the choice of channel and cadence. Your edits reads the same records directly to show what is in review, what went live, and what was declined. The standalone on-site inbox was retired when Your edits shipped, on purpose: two inboxes is one too many.

Each event points at where the real thing lives, never restates it. A review question is answered on the pull request, not in a reply to a notification, so there is one conversation, in public, with a permanent home.

The channel

Use the channel you already have

Channel choice lives in Settings, and the default leans on what a contributor already uses. A contributor with a connected GitHub account who picks GitHub gets GitHub's own notifications and no email from us at all. Otherwise, email. And off is a first-class choice for the contributor who would rather just open Your edits and look.

GitHubnative, no duplicate email

For a connected GitHub account. We lean entirely on GitHub's notifications and send nothing ourselves, so the loop is not doubled.

Email, or offthe fallback, and the opt-out

Email is the channel when GitHub is not chosen. Off sends nothing, and Your edits still shows every status. No channel is mandatory.

Where we draw the line

A status line, not a megaphone

Only the three lifecycle events. In review, live, declined. No digests, no tips, no re-engagement nudges. A notification means something happened to your edit, never that we want your attention.

The conversation lives on the pull request. Notifications link to the discussion, they are not the discussion. One public thread per change, not a private back-and-forth.

Off is a real option. A contributor can turn notifications off entirely and lose nothing, because Your edits still shows every status on demand.

Do not double the channel. A GitHub-native contributor gets GitHub's notifications and no email. We never send the same news twice.

Recommendation

What to hold

  1. Keep the event set to three. The value is that a notification always means a real change to a real edit. Adding marketing or digest events would spend that trust immediately.

  2. Point at the source, never copy it. Every event links to the PR or the device page. The notification is a pointer, so there is exactly one place the truth lives.

  3. Respect off, and respect GitHub. No channel is mandatory, and a connected contributor should never get a duplicate email. Lean on what they already have.
