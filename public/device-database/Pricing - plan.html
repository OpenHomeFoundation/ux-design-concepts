Planning · Pricing

Pricing: a fact first, a storefront maybe never

Two different things wear the word "price". One is a reference fact the manufacturer sets, citable and region-bound, that behaves exactly like a spec. The other is a live shop price in your country, and the moment money flows back through an affiliate link it stops being a fact and starts being a business. This plan keeps the two apart, ships the safe one, and treats the delicate one as a decision before it is a feature.

Reference price builds on Stage 04 · Variants and families · retail and affiliate are governed by the editorial stance

Split the word in two, and the placement answers itself. A reference price (MSRP) is a region-bound fact that rides the same axis as voltage and the EAN, so it is an increment on Stage 04, sitting right beside the variant data it depends on. Live retail pricing across shops in a reader's country is a different animal: it needs an outside data pipeline, it is per-country not per-region, and it carries a funding mechanism the editorial stance now permits only on strict terms. That is not an increment you quietly toggle. It is a separate, later stage, governed by those terms.

Where it slots · stage or increment

Run it through the same test the other stages use

The roadmap already has a test for this. A stage changes the core unit of the database. An increment builds on a single parent stage and leaves the unit alone. Price does not change the unit: a priced device is still a device, a priced variant still a variant. So price is an increment, never a stage, by the project's own rule. The only real question is which stage it hangs off, and the answer is the one that owns region.

Reference price depends on the region axis from Stage 04, the same way power does. Show a reader in Amsterdam the United States list price and you have published exactly the kind of wrong-region fact the variants plan refuses, the price equivalent of "230 V, Type F" shown in North America. So it cannot come before the region switcher. It comes right after it, as a sibling of the variant children. Live retail depends on that and on a funding decision, so it sits further out, behind Goal-based discovery, and moves only on the disclosure terms the stance now sets.

The honest read · what the updated stance allows

The stance no longer bans affiliate links, but the discipline still binds

This is the part to get right before any code. The editorial stance used to settle the question in a single sentence, and that sentence was part of why the project is trusted. It has been revised in the open, so the safeguard now lives in how the money behaves, not in a blanket ban.

What changed

The editorial stance used to forbid this outright. That clause has been removed, deliberately, and the page now reads, word for word:

"There are no sponsored placements and no recommendation rankings."

Dropping "no affiliate links" does not make affiliate income free of consequences. It moves the safeguard from a promise never to earn a referral fee to a promise that the fee can never bend a fact. The honesty of the About page is still the asset. Affiliate income stays a decision the Foundation owns in the open, disclosed on every surface it touches, never ranked by payout. A quiet contradiction is still the one outcome ruled out.

There is a version of this that stays true to the mission. Funding the upkeep of a neutral reference is a legitimate, even admirable, reason to earn a referral fee, and plenty of trusted independent catalogues do it openly. The line is not "affiliate or not". The line is whether the money can ever bend a fact. Keep price a dated, sourced fact shown the same for everyone, never sort shops by what they pay us, disclose the arrangement plainly on every surface it touches, and the arrangement can coexist with neutrality. Drop any one of those and it cannot.

The shape

Price is data on a variant, with a date and a source

Both layers share one rule borrowed straight from the variants plan: a value binds to the axis it varies on. Reference price binds to region. A retail observation binds to a country and, crucially, to a moment. A price with no date is a rumour. The shape carries the date so the page can be honest about how fresh, or how stale, the number is.

No date, no trust. // Each is a point-in-time snapshot, never presented as a live ticker. offer { gtin: "8719514291218", // the join key, already on the variant country: "NL", currency: "EUR", shop: "tweakers-partner", amount: 84.00, capturedAt: "2026-06-08", // drives the freshness label affiliate: true // disclosed wherever shown }

Notice the join key. The variant already carries a GTIN / EAN once Stage 04 binds identifiers to region. That single identifier is what lets a price attach to the right variant without anyone hand-listing prices per device, the exact pattern the trusted-sources plan uses to pull in outside data at scale. Price is just one more thing keyed on the EAN.

Getting prices, country by country, without the giant shops

Five sources, ranked by how neutral they are

The question was how to get pricing in different countries without leaning on the big marketplaces. The good news: the join key is already there. Stage 04 gives every regional variant a GTIN / EAN, and that is the one piece every price source in the world keys on. With that, sources stack from cleanest to most delicate. Take them in order, stop wherever the appetite for risk runs out.

Manufacturer list priceThe maker's own site or press kit, per region. The cleanest source for the reference layer. No shop involved, fully citable, changes rarely. Neutral
schema.org Offer microdataMost shops publish price and priceCurrency as standardised structured data on the product page. A neutral, machine-readable read of one number, keyed by GTIN, no marketplace API needed. Neutral
Community price reportsA contributor records "paid €84 at shop X, country NL, June 2026". Reviewed like any edit, shown dated and attributed. Sparse, but the most on-brand and fully Amazon-free. Neutral
Independent comparison enginesGeizhals, Idealo, Tweakers Pricewatch, PriceRunner, Kieskeurig and the like. Spec-first, per-country, with feeds or affiliate programmes. Proprietary and regional, so integrated one country at a time. Partner
Mission-aligned shops, directSpecialty smart-home and DIY retailers that run their own affiliate programmes and stock the hardware these readers actually buy. Chosen for fit, not for who pays most. This is where affiliate income would come from, not the marketplaces. Affiliate

A real difference from trusted sources is worth calling out. Those sources are reviewed by a human once, then trusted. A price is reviewed once and wrong by next week. So the calm sources, manufacturer list price and community reports, can flow through the existing review front door, because they are slow-moving or explicitly point-in-time. The fast sources, comparison engines and shop feeds, cannot go through human review on every refresh, so they must carry their own freshness machinery: a capturedAt on every number, a visible "seen N days ago" label, and a hard cutoff past which a price is hidden rather than shown stale.

Where we draw the line

Money can fund the truth, never bend it

The editorial stance is neutral, citable, never sponsored. Pricing is allowed to exist only inside that sentence, not around it. These rails hold at every step above.

A price is a dated, sourced fact, not a deal. Every number carries a currency, a source, and the date it was seen. No date, no display. A price shown without its freshness is the pricing version of an unverified claim.

Shops are never ranked by what they pay us. List them in a fixed neutral order, by country or by name, the same for every reader. The day the order follows commission is the day the page stops being a reference.

Disclosure is on the surface, not in a footer nobody reads. If a link is affiliate, it says so right where it sits, in the same plain terms the editorial stance now uses.

Price binds to the variant, like every other fact. Reference price to region, retail price to country, and never pooled across a family. A US plug's price never shows on the EU variant, the same scoping the variants plan applies to telemetry.

Recommendation

What to do, in order

  1. Treat pricing as an increment, never a stage. A priced device is still a device. By the project's own test it cannot be a stage. Reference price is increment 04.4 on Variants, retail is the gated Stage 06 behind Goal-based discovery.

  2. Reference price shipped, region binding is the open edge. It is live as a per-currency list on the device, reuses the review path, and carries almost no policy weight. The remaining work is to bind each price to a region once Stage 04 lands, ideally proven on a device where the regional price genuinely differs.

  3. Keep the affiliate discipline ahead of the feature. The stance has been revised in the open: the blanket ban is gone, a disclosure rule stands in its place. Ship affiliate links only with that disclosure on every surface, never ranked by payout. A quiet contradiction would cost more trust than the income could ever be worth.

  4. Key everything on the GTIN, and source from the calm end first. Manufacturer price, schema.org offers, and community reports get you real prices in many countries with zero dependence on the marketplaces. Reach for partner shops and feeds only when those run out.

  5. Put the date on every number. Freshness labels and a staleness cutoff are not polish, they are the line between a useful price and a rumour. Build them in from the first render, not after.
