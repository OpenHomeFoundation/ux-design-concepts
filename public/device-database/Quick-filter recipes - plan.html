Planning · Discovery

Quick-filter recipes: a step, not a destination

The six recipes in the search empty state are useful today, but they are intent-named, category-deep. This is the plan for what they are now, and the increments that grow them into the goal-based discovery sketched in the Ideas discussion.

Anchored to Discussion #147 · Beyond categories: presenting devices based on what visitors are trying to achieve

The recipes are the hardcoded seed of "use cases as a browse entry point." They belong in Foundation and should stay shipped, but they are not the end goal. Do not scale them by hand. Invest instead in the one cheap move that unlocks everything after: turning the list into a small data layer. Goal-based discovery is then a new stage built on top of it.

Today · Stage 01 Foundation

What a recipe actually is

A recipe is a small object. Its filters field maps one to one onto the Browse page's URL parameters, so clicking a row just navigates to #/browse?… with those params pre-filled. No new filter code path, and the live count is exactly what you land on.

{ title: "Sensors with local connection", breadcrumb: "Sensors · Occupancy and motion · Local connection", filters: { category: ["sensors", "presence"], localOnly: true } }

Six recipes ship. Four of six lean on localOnly: true. Five of six are a single category.

Here is the honest read. The title promises an intent. The engine is one category plus a local-connection toggle. There is no data behind a recipe, and nothing crosses a category boundary. That is genuinely fine for what they do now: a zero-typing on-ramp into Browse for the most common starting points. The limitation only matters once we want them to mean more.

The bigger thing they point at · Discussion #147

What a device is, versus what it is for

The discussion borrows a pattern from marketplaces: separate what a product is (a fixed fact) from where a buyer expects to find it (computed from intent). A contact sensor is a contact sensor. It is also a building block for a fridge-left-open alert, a door alert, or a leak-detection setup, depending on the goal. Recipes today only ever speak the left column.

What a device isfixed fact · the foundation

The category and reported behaviour. A motion sensor, a smart plug, a hub. Authored once, stable. This is what the database is built on, and it stays the source of truth.

What it is forcomputed intent · the frontier

The goals and setups a device shows up in. "Secure my doors and windows." "Monitor my energy use." Cross-category, curated at first, derived from real co-usage later.

Discussion #147 lists four expressions of the right-hand column: use cases as a browse entry point, a "used for" section on device pages, search that understands intent, and lightweight collections from co-occurrence data. The recipes are the embryonic, hand-authored version of the first one.

The path

One refactor, then a new stage

Recipes stay in Foundation. Everything beyond them becomes a proposed Stage 05, "Goal-based discovery," whose increments build on Foundation and ladder up in cost and ambition. Each one ships on its own and is a real improvement, not just scaffolding for the next.

Where we draw the line

A lens on facts, never a buying guide

Discussion #147 asks where the device database ends and a smart-home guide begins. The editorial stance answers it: neutral, citable, never sponsored. These guardrails hold at every increment above.

A use case filters facts, it does not rank them. No "best," no recommendation order, no affiliate. A goal is a saved query with a human name, not an opinion.

Curated first, computed later, never editorialized. Hand-authoring a goal catalog is fine. Writing tutorials or setup advice is out of scope, that is the guide, not the database.

The count never lies. The property that makes recipes trustworthy today, the number is exactly what you land on, must survive every increment.

Recommendation

What to do, in order

  1. Keep the six recipes shipped, and freeze the list. They earn their place as a zero-typing on-ramp. Adding more by hand is the wrong investment.

  2. Do 05.1 next. Move recipes into the data layer. It is cheap, ships invisibly, and is the prerequisite for everything else.

  3. Treat 05.2 as the real design problem. The first cross-category recipe is where "intent-named" finally becomes intent-driven. That is the increment worth a proper design pass.

  4. Open Stage 05 on the canvas when 05.1 lands. Name it "Goal-based discovery" and point it at Discussion #147, so the roadmap shows where the recipes are heading.
